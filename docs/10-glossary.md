# Glossary

Plain-English definitions for the terms this manual keeps using, plus a few marketplace words you'll meet on help pages.

## Photography terms

**Aspect ratio** — The shape of a photo as width : height (e.g., 1:1 square, 4:3, 16:9). Marketplace pages care about this more than you'd think; see [Per-marketplace size habits](07-marketplaces.md).

**Bounce / bounce card** — Any white surface angled to catch light at your item and throw it back into shadows. Foam board is the small-business version; see [Light without a rig](02-light.md).

**Blown highlights** — Parts of a photo so bright they're pure white with no detail left. Editors can't recover them, which is why slightly underexposing is safer than slightly overexposing (see [Light without a rig](02-light.md)).

**Diffusion** — Putting an even thin sheet (parchment paper, thin fabric) between your light and the item to soften edges and cut glare.

**Exposure** — How bright the photo is overall. On phones: tap the subject, then drag the sun icon up/down.

**Fill** — Light (from a bounce card or reflector) that softens the dark side of the item.

**Flat lay** — Shooting the item from directly above, lying flat. Standard for apparel measurements.

**Hard light / soft light** — Hard light (direct sun, bare bulbs) makes crisp-edged shadows; soft light (window, overcast sky) makes gentle, readable ones. Listing photos want soft.

**HDR (High Dynamic Range)** — A phone mode that merges multiple exposures. Handy for landscapes, moody for resale — see the caution in [Light without a rig](02-light.md).

**Main image / main photo** — The first photo in a listing, the one shown in search results. Each platform has the strictest rules here (Amazon especially).

**Overexposed / underexposed** — Too bright / too dark overall. Both can be partly edited back; only underexposure is recoverable without fakery.

**Scale prop** — A ruler, coin, tape measure, or known object photographed beside the item to prove its size; see [Backgrounds and scale](04-backgrounds.md).

**Seam / "seamless" background** — A backdrop curled so there's no line between wall and table (the studio look, $5 version).

**sRGB** — The standard web color space. Shoot and save in it; marketplace screens assume it.

**White balance (WB)** — Which shade of white the camera thinks white is. Wrong WB makes cream look yellow or white look blue — a top cause of "not as described" color disputes.

## Marketplace terms

**Dispute / "not as described" claim** — When a buyer tells the marketplace the item doesn't match its listing. The whose-fault question is answered almost entirely by comparing your photos to the returned item; this manual exists to keep that comparison boring (see [Honest condition shots](03-condition.md)).

**ASIN** — Amazon's product ID. Amazon listings attach to one, unless you create the page.

**Detail page** — Amazon's word for the full listing page (what buyers browse, what you edit through Seller Central).

**Etsy / eBay slot counts** — Photos allowed per listing: eBay up to 24 (most categories), Etsy up to 20.

**KDP** — Kindle Direct Publishing, Amazon's self-publishing arm. Ebook covers upload in their own dimensions (see [Per-marketplace size habits](07-marketplaces.md)).

**Listing suppression** — Platform slang for a listing being made un-searchable (Amazon does this for main-image violations).

**SKU** — Stock Keeping Unit: your own internal label for the item — also the sane start of your photo filename habit (see [Phone editing without fakery](06-editing.md)).

**Stock photo** — A manufacturer's or catalog image, not your own. Banned for used items on eBay; misleading everywhere else.

**sRGB vs CMYK** — Screen color vs print color. Screens (marketplaces, KDP ebook covers) want sRGB/RGB; print files (KDP paperback wraps, via KDP's template) are separate files.

## Cleaning / condition terms

**Before/after pair** — Two photos of the same surface, same spot, same angle: one as found, one cleaned; see [Before/after cleaning shots](05-cleaning.md).

**Disclosure photo** — A close-up photographing a flaw straight-on with a size reference. The phrase of the year for this manual; see [Honest condition shots](03-condition.md).

**Etching** — Permanent acid/hard-water damage on glass (vs limescale, which comes off). Even a great clean can't remove etching, which is exactly why a before/after pair is worth shooting when glass is involved.

**Ghost mannequin / flat** — Amazon-approved alternatives for clothing main images when a model isn't available.

**Mint / not-mint** — Collectibles grade language. Say "not mint" honestly and photograph the specific flaw rather than claiming grades you can't defend (see [Shot-list cards](08-shot-lists.md)).

**Shelf wear** — Condition wear you'd see *before* opening — box rubs, faded edges. Often what in-box buyers judge; photograph it clearly.

**Tarnish** — Metal dulling that polish reverses (vs scratches, which it doesn't — and which is why the before/after pair exists).

## Workflow terms

**Batch** — Photographing several items in one session under one lighting setup. See "For low-energy days" in the [editing checklist](09-editing-checklist.md).

**Card (shot-list card)** — A fixed list of shots per item category, designed to be repeated: [Shot-list cards](08-shot-lists.md).

**Corner lab** — The small-business photo setup this manual describes: table, window, backdrop, bounce, done — see [The corner lab](01-corner-lab.md).

**Preset** — A saved bundle of edits. This manual's advice: none for listing photos — see why in [Phone editing without fakery](06-editing.md).
