# Honest Condition Shots That Cut Disputes

Nothing drops a small seller's account standing faster than "not as described" claims. Nothing prevents them better or more cheaply than **a photo of exactly the flaw, taken in honest light, with a normal measurement.**

## The rule that keeps you out of disputes

**Assume no one reads your description.** The buyer is deciding from photos. So the photos must carry the whole story: the good, the used, the worn.

A photo that flatters the item and hides a flaw is only half a lie — and the marketplace will treat it as a whole one when the buyer opens the case. A photo that shows the flaw straight on is a favor you did *yourself*.

## The flaw-briefing protocol

For every item, hold it in your hands and ask: *what would I want to know if I were buying this?* Anything you would call out on the phone to a friend ("works fine, but the ears are a bit bent on the left one") gets its own photo.

1. **Find each flaw.** Good light, close range, both hands, both sides.
2. **Photograph it straight-on** (not at a flattering angle, not from directly above).
3. **Add a reference** — a fingertip, a coin, a ruler — so the flaw's *size* is obvious.
4. **Photograph the normal parts too.** A flaw in frame 14 of 18 reads as carefully disclosed. A flaw alone in frame 2 reads as the whole item.
5. **Keep the flaw list in the description too**, so the two reinforce each other rather than contradict.

Flaws that deserve their own photo every time:

- Scratches, dents, chips, stains (and old repairs)
- Missing pieces, missing hardware, replaced parts
- Odor-sensitive items: "vintage smell" is worth acknowledging honestly
- Anything you'd hesitate about if you received it as the buyer

## Don't fake condition — don't fake cleanliness either

"Cleaning" is great; hiding a flaw behind editing is not (see [Phone editing without fakery](06-editing.md)). If you cleaned an item, save a quick "before" photo. Buyers trust a seller who shows the work. See [Before/after cleaning shots](05-cleaning.md) for the right way.

## The pre-listing read-through test

Before you upload, do this one-minute check on your own listing as if you were the buyer:

- Can I see the whole item in the first photo?
- Is there a photo showing the *actual size* against something real?
- Is every single flaw visible here — and hard to miss?
- Would I be surprised in any way unboxing this?

If the answer to the last one is "no," the listing is ready.

## A hidden benefit: fewer questions

Honest photos don't just prevent disputes. They pre-qualify buyers. A buyer who has seen the exact scratch and wants it anyway is the *best kind* of buyer — one who won't be disappointed later.
