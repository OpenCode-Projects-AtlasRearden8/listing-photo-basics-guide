Shot-List Cards

Print one card, tape it inside your corner (see The corner lab), and work down it for every listing in that category. Consistency across items is what makes your shop feel like a shop. Slot numbers assume eBay/Etsy-style multi-photo listings; on Amazon, “main” images follow the stricter main-image rules first.

Each card follows the same rhythm: overview → all sides → details → flaws → scale → proof of life.

Card: Apparel and clothing

# Shot Why
1 Front, flat or on hanger, clean background The click-getter
2 Back, same angle/distance Full shape
3 Front, on a hanger or a real-style shot Shows how it hangs in use
4 Flat with tape measure across chest Size proof
5 Flat with tape measure for length, sleeve, inseam Inseam-flat shots; fills buyer measuring guides
6 Fabric close-up (weave, thread, sheen) Quality note
7 Tag: brand, size, materials, care Buyers shop from tags
8–10 Flaw close-ups, each with fingertip or coin for scale Honest condition photos (see Honest condition shots)

Tips: flat lay on a foam board keeps sleeves symmetrical; steam wrinkles first — wrinkles photograph as “used harder than it is.”

Card: Books and media

# Shot Why
1 Cover, straight-on, filling frame Click-getter
2 Spine, straight-on Age and shelf-wear read from spine
3 Back cover + any page-edge spotting Full condition context
4 Page stack from above Readability, clean pages
5 Inside pages at random middle spread Text condition; “no highlighting” proof
6 Any flaw: torn dustjacket, marker, loose binding — close-up Disclosure
7 ISBN page (copyright page) Edition identification
8 Book next to a ruler, or flat with the tape Size proof

Tips: open one page across a bounce card to flatten the page curve; if there’s a price/sticker, either peel cleanly or show the residue honestly in a close-up.

Card: Electronics

# Shot Why
1 Front, screen-on if it helps (only if screen is good) Click-getter
2 All sides, one each (4 shots) Full condition
3 Screen close-up, powered on Works like it should
4 Ports, buttons, and charge port close-up Wear spots for used electronics
5 Accessories in one shot: cables, remotes, manuals included Full “what’s here”
6 Flaw close-ups: casing scratches, dents, wear Disclosure
7 Serial/label while the device works — this is optional; blur or skip if it printed your data Privacy; also used for ID checks

Tips: photograph powered-off for the general shots, powered-on for the screen shot; never shoot with personal info on screen — logout and wipe before photos, not after shipping. List “tested working” in words; photos alone don’t prove function.

Card: Glassware, dishes, fragile goods

# Shot Why
1 Front or three-quarter angle, lit for no-blare Click-getter; glass wants soft light (see Light without a rig)
2 Top-down overview Set/pattern visible
3 Side profile with ruler Size + dimensions
4 Maker’s-mark or label close-up Identification value
5 Rim of each piece, close-up Chips live on rims; the #1 complaint
6 Underside of each piece Hairlines, utensil marks inside bowls
7–9 Individual pieces for sets/multiples Buyers of sets appreciably inspect each

Tips: dark or mid-tone backdrop strips glassware’s ghost shapes; bounce side strips “light-line” definition. Never leave glass on cloth with tiny fibers — under window light they hog focus.

Card: Jewelry and small valuables

# Shot Why
1 Straight-on, soft-light, tape measure or coin laid beside (in-focus) Click-getter + size in one
2 Three-quarter view Depth and dimension
3 Stamp/mark/karat stamp close-up Authentics
4 Clasps, backs, fasteners Working hardware
5 Stone/crystal close-up under diffused light Inclusions honestly shown
6 Flaw close-ups: scratches, wear plating Disclosure
7 In/on your hand (if selling style, not material) Scale + style
8 In its box or pouch, if included “What arrives”

Tips: the metal’s own shine lies; your job is just to let it shine in frame without glare — diffuse window. Never shoot on a dark towel (lint shows as gray dust) — use tight-knit or smooth board.

Card: Furniture and large items

If it can’t come inside, shoot it outside on an overcast day — outdoor soft light beats indoor bounce, and buyers get to see true color.

# Shot Why
1 Full view, corner-on angle so two sides show Click-getter
2 Straight-on front, steel level Symmetry check
3 Each face: top, sides, back if relevant Full condition
4 Legs, runners, hinges close-up Where the wear lives
5 Underside and interior shots (drawers open) Hidden-state honesty
6 Fabric/finish close-up under soft light Condition of surfaces
7 Dimensions laid out: height, width, depth, seat height, in flat-lay style Size proof
8 Any damage, at close range with tape Disclosure

Tips: if you can’t move it, clean the area around it — even outdoors, a visual wipe-down of leaves and yard dust counts; never shoot body-down-hallway shots past your own door.

Card: Toys, boxy collectibles, in-box items

# Shot Why
1 Front of box, filling frame Click-getter
2 Box sides + top/bottom (2–4 shots) Shelf wear
3 Interior if opened: item inside with accessories sorted “What’s included”
4 Item out of box, front and back Condition of thing itself
5 Accessories individually or lined up in one shot Count check
6 Copyright/date stamps, model numbers Identification
7 Box flaws in close-up: crush, dent, sun-fade In-box collectibles value rides here
8 With ruler next to box Scale

Tips: never claim “Mint in Box” unless everything really is — a “not-mint” disclosure photo costs a bit of polish and saves a whole return (see Honest condition shots).

Card: Handmade and craft items

# Shot Why
1 Straight-on on clean background Click-getter
2 Life shot: in use, in context, worn/on-body where relevant Etsy’s selling shot
3 Craftsperson close-up: stitch detail, joinery, glaze The proof it’s handmade
4 Size on hand or next to standard object Scale
5 Packaging or how it ships Expectation-setting
6 Variation shots if multiples/colors exist Shoppers shop variation
7 Wrist/hand for wearables Wearability

Tips: handmade is the one category where a styled “in context” scene reliably helps rather than distracts — but the item stays the brightest, sharpest thing (see Backgrounds and scale).

Building your own card

When you hit a new category, build a card from these five steps:

  1. Overview — one to four sides, clean background, filled frame.
  2. Function — do the moving/closing/locking parts work on camera.
  3. Identification — marks, serials, model numbers, copyright stamps.
  4. Scale — ruler, coin, tape, or a known object in the same plane.
  5. Disclosure — every flaw, close-up, with a referee (fingertip, coin, ruler) for size.

If a shot list ever mixes you up, the editing checklist catches anything missed before upload.
