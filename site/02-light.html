Light Without a Rig

Good light is the entire trick of listing photography. Not gear, not skill — light. Get this page right and most of your photos will already look professional.

The single rule

One big, soft light source, coming from one main direction, plus something white to bounce it back into the shadows.

That’s it. A window plus a piece of foam board beats a $900 softbox setup for most resale items.

Use the window like a softbox

  • Perpendicular table setup: table against the side wall of the window, so light walks across the item at 45° and skims texture gently.
  • Distance is your dimmer: move the item toward or away from the window to change brightness without touching a setting.
  • Stepping back makes light softer too: the bigger the light source looks from the item’s point of view, the softer the shadows. A window inches away is crisp and dramatic; a window three feet away, partly covered by a curtain, is even and soft.
  • Cloudy days are a gift. The whole sky becomes the light. Shoot your biggest batches then.
  • Avoid direct sun on the item unless you want hard shadows (about 10am or mid-afternoon light is usually steady and bright; noon direct sun is the worst of both).

Bounce: your free shadow-lifting tool

Shadows that go full black look like missing product information. Fill them:

  • White foam board, white paper, a white pillowcase, even a white wall — lean it or tape it on the far side of the item, angled to catch window light and toss it back.
  • For a single small item on the table, a sheet of white paper curved into a shallow “U” (like a little half-pipe) is a bounce and a mini-studio in one.
  • For glassware and dark bottles, bounce the gradient across the glass — a strip of light down one side — by moving the board closer and farther.

Diffuse: your free glare tool

Shiny things (glossy book covers, varnished wood, plastic) glare under raw window light:

  • Parchment paper or thin white fabric taped across the window turns hot light into even light.
  • Change your angle, not the light: glare moves with the camera. If glare sits on the item’s shoulder, step right or lower the phone.
  • A second bounce on the dark side can balance a reflective surface so the whole item is readable.

What the phone can help with (and what it can’t)

  • Tap to focus on the item, then drag the sun icon down until highlights are just at the edge of “still bright.” Slightly under-exposed beats slightly blown-out: blown highlights are gone — no editor will bring them back.
  • Lock exposure and focus (long-press on most phones) so nothing refocuses between shots.
  • HDR rarely likes resale items. It merges exposures at odd weights; if you must, take both a native shot and an HDR shot and pick.

Hard-mode items, quick answers

Thing that’s tricky Quick fix
Glassware (glare on every surface) Dark or mid-tone backdrop strips the glare plus bounce for the gradient
Shiny metal (jewelry, tools) Diffuse the window; black side of a board kills ghost reflections
Dark colors (navy, black fabric) Reduce exposure (tap and drag down), bounce light onto the item itself
White items on white backdrop Slide a gray/colored backdrop behind so the item doesn’t disappear
Textured fabric (knits, denim) Window light across the surface, not onto it — brings up texture
Long items (yard furniture, brooms) Shoot outside on an overcast day instead; indoor bounce won’t reach both ends

Time-of-day habits worth keeping

  • North light or all-day-shade windows are most consistent across hours — good for matching shots done over multiple days.
  • Batch around the light. When light is nice, photograph five items, not one.
  • Same time of day across sessions for items in the same listing (like “left and right shoe” pairs) so brightness matches.

Light wins matches. The next three pages — condition, backgrounds, cleaning — all sit on top of it.
