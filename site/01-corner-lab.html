The Corner Lab: On Any Budget

You do not need a studio. You need a corner — four square feet you can dedicate to photos, with a wall and a window you trust. Set it up once. Every future listing becomes ten minutes instead of an afternoon.

Budget tier 1: The $0 lab

Everything here can be found in most homes today:

  • One window. North-facing is famous for being soft and steady, but any window works for part of the day (see Light without a rig).
  • A table pushed against the wall. Kitchen table; desk; ironing board if that’s what exists.
  • A clean bedsheet, pillowcase, or poster board as a backdrop when a clean background helps (see Backgrounds and scale).
  • A chair or wall shelf as your tripod: prop the phone against a stack of books, lean a cutting board behind it, use the timer so nobody shakes the phone.
  • A dollar-store ruler or tape measure for scale shots.
  • Household light tools: a sheet of white paper taped at an angle bounces light into shadows. A piece of parchment paper taped over a bright lamp softens it.

The $0 lab’s secret is the window plus a white surface one step away. Bounce and diffuse, and you’ve eliminated 80% of the “used-gear listing that looks like a crime scene” problem.

Budget tier 2: The $25 lab

Adds a few durable items, most from a craft, discount, or big-box store:

Item Rough cost What it fixes
Foam board sheets (2–3) ~$5 Ever-clean backdrops and bounce cards
Small 5-in-1 reflector (43in) ~$15–20 Fills shadows without a single bulb
Painter’s tape ~$4 Holds backdrops flat; pulls off cleanly
Plastic stacking tub reuse Keeps the corner a one-minute setup

Foam board is the workhorse of small resale photography: white side up for bounce, black side as a dark frame behind shiny items, one board leaning to diffuse direct light.

Budget tier 3: The $75 lab

Beyond $75, returns get small, so spend only on:

  • Two clamp lights + soft-white LED bulbs (~$20): replaces daylight on dark/rainy days. Keep them behind and above the item, bounced off foam board — never pointed straight at it.
  • A phone tripod (~$20): a real time-saver for overhead flat-lays and consistent shot lists.
  • One small light tent (40cm) (~$20): genuinely useful for glassware, reflective tools, and jewelry. Photos every other day? Skip it and bounce harder.

Anything past this — softboxes, studio strobes, turntables — is nice-to-have. Based on how successful small listings look to buyers, none of it is needed.

Setting up the corner

  1. Find the window wall. Put the table perpendicular to the window, item between window and wall.
  2. Clear the background to bare wall. If the wall is busy, hang a sheet or pin a poster board; laser-flat its top edge with painter’s tape.
  3. Place the item ~2–3 feet from the backdrop so backdrop shadows don’t sit behind it.
  4. Stand where the light comes from: shoot with the window at your back or at a 45° angle. Never toward the window (unless you want a silhouette, and you don’t).
  5. Set your phone height so the item is level with the camera’s plane — tilt only for deliberate angle shots.
  6. Write the setup down. Same table, same two boards, same angle every time means Listing #47 looks like Listing #3. That consistency is what makes small shops look like a shop.

Make it repeatable

The corner lab earns its keep by being the same every time. Consider a rolling checklist taped to the wall inside the corner:

  • Boards wiped, sheet shaken out, backdrop square
  • Window light available, or bounce cards ready
  • Phone battery charged, lens wiped
  • Ruler on the table for scale shots
  • Timer set on the phone (3-second enough for a propped camera)

One layout, one lighting plan, and wipe-and-reshoot becomes your normal. And that matters for the next chapters, because light is everything: Light without a rig.
